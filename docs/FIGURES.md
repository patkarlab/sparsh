# Figures of the model

The figures describe a model and the arrays it was trained on: a t-SNE map, heatmaps of the CpGs that mark each class, the global methylation of each class, maps of the regions that are differentially methylated in each class, the F1 score of each class in cross-validation, and a schematic of the training and the network. They are made in six steps, tsne, markers, performance, schematic, the R script figures/dmr.R and regions, which one batch job runs in order. The figures of the arrays need a training run together with the training arrays it lists, so they can be made only by those who hold the arrays; the F1 scores and the schematic need only the run folder:

```bash
python scripts/cpg_annotation.py --manifest infinium-methylationepic-v-1-0-b5-manifest-file.csv \
    --out cpg_annotation_hg19.tsv.gz
conda env create -f envs/figures_r.yml
qsub -v RUN_NAME=v04_labels,ANNOTATION=/path/to/cpg_annotation_hg19.tsv.gz jobs/figures.pbs
```

The steps can also be run one by one; `python scripts/figures.py <step> --help` lists the options of a step.

## Arrays and annotation

The arrays are exactly those the model was trained on. A training run lists them in folds.csv with the class each was given after the label map and all exclusions, and names in config.json the pickle the values came from. The arrays are read from that pickle once and kept in a cache folder next to the figures. Arrays of classes left out of the model, such as acute myeloid leukaemia with myelodysplasia-related changes and with MECOM rearrangement, can be added to a second, supplementary t-SNE map. They are chosen from the same pickle with the run's own label map and exclusion list, so an array left out of training on purpose stays out. All other figures use the model's arrays only.

CpGs are placed on the genome with Illumina's manifest of the MethylationEPIC array (version 1.0 B5), in GRCh37 (hg19) coordinates, with the genes and the relation to CpG islands the manifest gives. scripts/cpg_annotation.py turns the manifest into the table the figures read. The colours and the order of the classes come from configs/class_colours.json and configs/class_hierarchy.json, so the figures use the colours of the reports. The names come from configs/figure_names.json, which gives every class a short name in the notation and spelling of the manuscript (PML::RARA, HOX-dysregulated, Aneuploid) and every lineage the name that heads its classes in the legends; a class it does not list keeps its name in configs/display_names.json. Where two classes share a short name, as KMT2A-rearranged does in acute myeloid and B-lymphoblastic leukaemia, the maps and axes add the lineage in brackets.

## t-SNE map

The map uses the 5,000 CpGs with the largest standard deviation of beta values across the arrays. CpGs on the X and Y chromosomes, whose methylation follows sex, and CpGs missing in more than 5% of the arrays are left out; the few remaining missing values of a CpG are filled with its mean. The distance between two arrays is one minus the Pearson correlation of their values over these CpGs. The map is computed from the distance matrix with t-SNE (van der Maaten and Hinton, 2008) as implemented in scikit-learn, with a perplexity of 30, 2,000 iterations, random initialisation and a fixed seed of 42. The step writes the map coloured by class with a legend, the same with the name of each class written beside its arrays, the map coloured by lineage, the coordinates of every array, the list of CpGs used and the settings. On the labelled map the names are placed from the largest class down, each at the free position nearest the median of its arrays that overlaps no other name, covers few arrays of other classes and draws no line across another name or line; a name placed away from its arrays is joined to their median by a thin line. With classes outside the model added, the same set is written again with the prefix tsne_with_excluded_classes, and the added arrays are outlined in black.

## Class statistics and signature heatmap

A class is compared with other arrays CpG by CpG. The difference is taken in mean beta value, and the test is Welch's t test on M-values (the log2 ratio of beta to one minus beta, after clipping beta to 0.001 to 0.999). The false discovery rate over all CpGs follows Benjamini and Hochberg (1995). Missing values are left out CpG by CpG. For the region maps and for class_markers_summary.csv, which counts for every class the CpGs whose mean beta differs by at least 0.2 at a false discovery rate below 0.05, each class is compared with all other arrays of the model.

The signature heatmap is drawn as two images, one for each group of lineages in configs/figure_names.json: acute myeloid leukaemia together with acute leukaemia of ambiguous lineage, blastic plasmacytoid dendritic cell neoplasm and normal bone marrow (signature_heatmap_myeloid), and B- and T-lymphoblastic leukaemia (signature_heatmap_lymphoblastic). Within an image each class is compared with the other arrays of the same image, so the heatmap shows what separates the classes of those lineages rather than the lineages themselves. The signature CpGs of a class are, among CpGs off the sex chromosomes whose mean beta differs by at least 0.2 between the class and the rest of its image, the 25 with the largest positive t statistic and the 25 with the largest negative one. A CpG chosen for more than one class is shown once, in the block of the first class in the legend order. The heatmap shows their beta values in every array of the image, with rows in blocks by the class they mark and columns grouped by class. Within a class the arrays are ordered by their signature score, the mean beta of the class's hypermethylated signature CpGs minus that of its hypomethylated ones. The CpGs chosen for an image, with their statistics and genes, are written to signature_cpgs_<group>.csv, and the order of its arrays to signature_heatmap_<group>_columns.csv.

Global methylation is the mean beta over all CpGs of an array, drawn per class as a box plot with the arrays as points.

## Differentially methylated regions

Regions are found with DMRcate (Peters and colleagues, Epigenetics and Chromatin, 2015) from the class statistics above. For every class, dmr.R places the autosomal CpGs on the genome and smooths their t statistics with a Gaussian kernel, using the DMRcate defaults for arrays (lambda 1,000 bases, C 2). It then joins neighbouring significant CpGs into regions. A CpG counts as individually significant when its false discovery rate is below 0.05 and its mean beta differs by at least 0.1. The second condition matters with thousands of arrays, where very small differences reach significance, because DMRcate sets its cut-off on the smoothed statistics from the number of individually significant CpGs. For each region DMRcate gives the number of CpGs, the smallest smoothed false discovery rate, three combined measures of significance and the largest and mean differences in beta.

The maps keep regions of at least three CpGs whose mean difference in beta is at least 0.2. dmr_map shows, class by class, where along the genome the class is hyper- or hypomethylated against all other arrays, and dmr_counts how many regions of each kind every class has. dmr_loci shows the strongest region of each class, the one with the smallest smoothed false discovery rate and then the largest difference, with the mean beta of every class at each CpG of the region and 2 kb around it. All regions, with the genes of their CpGs and whether they pass the filters, are in dmrs_all.tsv, and the versions of R and its packages in regions/sessionInfo.txt.

## F1 score by class

The step performance draws the F1 score of every class in the cross-validation of the training run, in the manner of Figure 1c of Sturgeon (Vermeulen and colleagues, Nature 2023). Every array was scored once, by the network of the fold that held it out, under fixed conditions, and the run keeps the predictions of each condition in cv_predictions_<condition>.csv. For a class, precision is the share of the arrays predicted as the class that belong to it, recall the share of its arrays predicted as the class, and F1 their harmonic mean, all on the most probable class without a threshold. The figure shows two bars for every class, the full arrays (the condition dense) in a light shade and simulated nanopore reads covering 30% of the model's CpGs without dilution (binary_0.30, the reference condition of the model card) in the full colour, with the number of arrays of the class after its name. Other conditions can be given with --conditions, for example binary-blast30_0.30 for the same coverage at 30% blasts. f1_by_class.csv holds the precision, recall and F1 of every class and condition.

## Schematic of the training and the network

The step schematic draws the training and the network of the model in three panels: the training arrays and the simulated nanopore input (dilution with normal marrow, reads covering part of the CpGs, the input encoding), the network with its layers, and the cross-validation with the model that was released. Every number in it is read from the run's config.json: the arrays, classes and CpGs, the share of draws diluted and the range of blast fractions, the range of coverage, the layer sizes, dropout and activation, the loss, the sampler and the optimiser, the patience of early stopping, the folds and the inner validation split, and the threshold of the calls. The small heatmaps that illustrate the steps are drawn from random numbers and stand for no real array.

## Checking

tests/test_figures.sh runs every step on synthetic arrays whose class signatures are known. It checks that the maps use the model's arrays, add only the requested classes and leave out the sex chromosomes. It checks that each heatmap holds the classes of its group and that the signature CpGs and the regions recover the planted signatures in the right direction, and it recomputes the F1 scores from the predictions. The region steps run when R with DMRcate is available (RSCRIPT=/path/to/Rscript).
